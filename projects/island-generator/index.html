What you're looking at

Every point on the sheet gets a height from Perlin noise, a smooth random function that looks like terrain. Heights are cut into steps, so each step reads as a contour band, and everything below the sea level is water.

That's the whole landscape. The same few sliders take it anywhere:

Land and sea

Land is pulled up around island centres. With Islands, around each centre; with Coast, towards one side of the sheet; with Spine, along a line through the centres. Island factor sets how far that pull reaches and Land weight how strong it is: at 0 the land runs to every edge, like a mainland. One centre gives an island, many small ones an archipelago.

Terrain details

Feature smoothness sets how broad the landforms are. Surface roughness adds finer detail on top. Ridges fold the terrain into sharp, branching crests. Coast warp bends everything so coastlines wander. Elevation exponent raises heights to a power: low gives plateaus, high gives sharp peaks, and the coast stays put.
