What you're looking at

Every point on the page gets a height from Perlin noise, a smooth random function that looks like terrain. Heights are then cut into steps, so each step reads as a contour band, and everything below the sea level is water.

That's the whole landscape. The same few sliders take it anywhere:

From island to mainland

Land focus blends the noise with the distance to one or more island centres. Near a centre the land is lifted, far away it sinks. At 0 the noise runs off every edge (a mainland); high, with one centre, it gathers into one island; with many small ones, an archipelago. Coast lifts one side of the page instead; Spine lifts a line — with Ridges up, a mountain range.

The texture

Feature scale is how many hills fit across the page. Roughness adds finer layers of noise on top. Ridges folds the noise into sharp crests. Coast warp bends the whole field so coastlines wander. Peakiness raises heights to a power: low gives plateaus, high gives spires, and the coast stays put.

Sea level

Sea level is set as how much of the sheet is under water, so it means the same thing whatever the other sliders do.
