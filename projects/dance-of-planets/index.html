What you're looking at

Two planets go around the Sun, each at its own speed. Every so often — a few days, in planet time — a line is drawn between them. That's all. Nothing else decides the picture except how long each planet takes to go around.

If one planet's year fits neatly into the other's, the pattern closes on itself and repeats. The closer the fit, the cleaner the shape.

The Dance of Venus

In 8 years, Earth goes around the Sun 8 times and Venus goes around 13 times — almost exactly. 13 − 8 = 5, so the pattern has five-fold symmetry. Because the fit is so nearly exact (Venus falls short by about 2°), the rose closes on itself and turns only very slowly, cycle after cycle.

Every pair has its own ratio. Jupiter and Saturn meet every 20 years in three places — a triangle. Neptune and Pluto are locked at exactly 3 : 2.

Lines and Trail

Lines draws the line between the two planets. Trail follows the point halfway between them instead — which for Earth and Venus traces the same shape as Venus's path as seen from Earth.
